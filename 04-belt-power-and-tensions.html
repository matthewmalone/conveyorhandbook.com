1. Belt Power and Tensions

Conveyor drive power is determined by a force balance. At constant belt speed, the drive pulley force equals the sum of all resistances opposing belt motion around the conveyor loop.

2. Conveyor Resistances

ISO 5048 / DIN 22101 sum the main, secondary, special and slope resistances, and the Conveyor Equipment Manufacturers Association (CEMA) method sums the same losses as separate component terms. The table below groups these resistances by distribution, mechanical origin and governing dependencies, with the main resistance divided into its no-load and loaded parts.

3. Effective Tension and Power Calculation

Effective tension, T_e, is the net peripheral force required to drive or brake the loaded conveyor, equal to the sum of all resistances resolved at the drive pulley (F_U in ISO 5048).

Engineering Takeaway: At a fixed mass flow, the load and lift components of effective tension vary inversely with belt speed, so their power demand is independent of speed, while empty running power rises in proportion to speed. Size the motor from P divided by the drive train efficiency, at the peak effective tension of the governing load case.

Note: Apply a slip safety factor of 1.2 or greater to T2_min and evaluate the worst case of a wet or cold pulley at peak starting and braking effective tension.

Engineering Takeaway: Set the take-up so that slack-side tension is not less than T2_min with the slip safety factor applied, and belt tension at the lowest-tension point of the carry and return runs is not less than the tension needed to hold the sag limit. On horizontal and low-lift conveyors the greater of the two sets the take-up force, and any excess slack-side tension adds directly to T1 and the required belt rating.

Engineering Takeaway: The minimum tight-side tension is C_w * T_e. A higher friction coefficient or a larger wrap angle lowers C_w, and with it the tight-side tension and belt rating needed to transmit a given effective tension.
