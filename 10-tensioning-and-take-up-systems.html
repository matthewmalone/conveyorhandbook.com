1. Tensioning and Take-Up Systems

The take-up system maintains slack-side belt tension (T2) to prevent drive pulley slip and absorbs belt elongation.

2. Elongation Characteristics and Take-Up Implications

Elastic elongation (stretch under load) and permanent elongation (constructional stretch during run-in) are the principal components of take-up travel. Where travel is insufficient, the carriage reaches its end stop, T2 falls and the drive pulley slips.

5. Calculating Minimum Loop Take-Up Travel

Minimum take-up travel is the sum of the components in the table below.

Table 5: Loop Take-Up Minimum Travel Components
Parameter Component Name Description Typical Values
TU_elasElastic elongation allowanceAbsorbs reversible elastic belt stretch caused by varying operating tensions (e.g. starting, stopping, loaded versus empty).Typically 0.10% to 2.0% of belt length, depending on carcass type. Carriage travel required is belt stretch divided by the number of belt strands, N. For a standard 2-strand loop, 1.0 m [3.3 ft] of carriage travel absorbs 2.0 m [6.6 ft] of belt stretch.
TU_permPermanent elongation allowanceAbsorbs irreversible constructional carcass stretch that occurs during the belt's initial run-in period.Typically 0.15% to 2.5% of total belt length for textile and solid woven belts, and 0.10% to 0.25% for steel cord belts. TU_safeSafety buffer at each end of travelMaintains physical clearance between the moving carriage and structural end stops during dynamic surges (e.g. emergency stops).0.2 to 2.0 m [8 to 79 in] minimum physical clearance required at both the forward and reverse travel limits to prevent mechanical impact.

5.1 Elastic Elongation Allowance

Engineering Takeaway: The allowance is based on C rather than on belt circuit length because the circuit is approximately 2 times C and a single-pulley take-up absorbs twice its travel in belt length. Applying elongation at rated working tension over the full belt length is conservative, as the average belt tension is lower.

5.2 Permanent Elongation Allowance

Engineering Takeaway: Permanent elongation occurs progressively, typically over the first 3 to 6 months of operation, as the carcass weave or cable geometry settles under tension. For cotton and nylon carcasses, permanent elongation equals or exceeds elastic elongation and can govern take-up travel. For steel cord belts, permanent elongation is small as a percentage but amounts to several metres of carriage travel on long overland conveyors.

5.3 Splice Length Allowance

Splicing progressively reduces total belt length. Provide sufficient travel to absorb this reduction without relocating the take-up pulley.

For surface conveyors with a design life of 15 to 20 years, the number of planned splices is typically 6 to 12 for a single-circuit belt. For underground development conveyors at high face advance rates, the planned splices can exceed 50 over a 12-month cycle.

Engineering Takeaway: Omitting the splice allowance from the take-up specification causes the carriage to reach its end stop before the end of belt life, particularly on systems with long splices and high splice frequencies.

Note: A splice that replaces an existing splice shortens the belt loop by approximately 2 * L_splice, because the old splice is cut out before the new one is made. Use 2 * L_splice for each planned replacement splice.

Engineering Takeaway: Bias length rises with belt width and can exceed the total step length on wide belts with few plies. A 4-ply belt 1,200 mm [48 in] wide with 300 mm [12 in] steps and a 0.3 bias consumes 3 * 0.30 + 0.3 * 1.2 = 1.26 m [4.1 ft] per splice.

5.4 Safety Buffer at Each End of Travel

The extended-end buffer (carriage at maximum forward travel with belt at maximum elongation) provides clearance for peak elastic elongation, thermal expansion, and carcass variability. Contact with the extended-end stop transfers the take-up force to the stop, reducing T2 and causing drive slip.

The retracted-end buffer (carriage at maximum rearward travel with belt at minimum elongation) accommodates an un-elongated new belt and dynamic starting transients. Contact with the retracted-end stop makes the take-up rigid and causes tension spikes during drive acceleration or deceleration.

Typical minimum safety buffers at each end of travel:

  • Screw take-up: 200 to 400 mm [8 to 16 in] each end
  • Gravity take-up: 400 to 600 mm [16 to 24 in] each end
  • Hydraulic or pneumatic take-up: 300 to 500 mm [12 to 20 in] each end
  • Fixed winch: 400 to 600 mm [16 to 24 in] each end
  • Semi-active winch: 500 to 1,000 mm [20 to 39 in] each end
  • Fully active winch: 800 to 1,500 mm [31 to 59 in] each end
  • Multilayer loop take-up: 1,000 to 2,000 mm [39 to 79 in] each end

On active winch systems, the extended-end safety buffer functions as a response-time reserve. Size d_ext so that the time for the carriage to traverse it exceeds the combined PID loop response and mechanical brake release times. For example, at a carriage velocity of 0.5 m/s [100 fpm], a 1.0 m [39 in] buffer provides 1.0 / 0.5 = 2.0 s of response time before end-stop contact.

Engineering Takeaway: For marginal response-time budgets, increase the safety buffer rather than retuning the PID loop aggressively, which can induce sustained carriage oscillation and accelerate component wear.

Engineering Takeaway: This calculation assumes a reference temperature of 20 deg C [68 deg F]. For elevated or sub-zero environments, adjust TU_min for the temperature dependence of carcass elongation and belt length. 5.6 Total Minimum Take-Up Travel for a Multilayer Configuration

Underground development conveyors with a multilayer loop take-up require internal belt storage capacity to sustain face advance operations between planned splices. This requirement is independent of elongation and splice parameters and adds to them in the total carriage travel.

Engineering Takeaway: Each metre of conveyor extension draws 2 m of belt from storage. Lengthening the interval between splice events reduces splicing frequency but increases BSC and the required carriage travel.

Engineering Takeaway: In a multilayer loop take-up, carriage translation absorbs N times its displacement in belt length, compared to the 2 times multiplier of a standard single-pulley configuration. Safety clearances (d_ext + d_ret) are physical carriage position limits on the rails and are added directly without division by N. Base all elongation and splice calculations on the total belt circuit length at maximum development extent.
