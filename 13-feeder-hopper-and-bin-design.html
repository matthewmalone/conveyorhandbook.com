1. Feeder, Hopper and Bin Design

Feeders extract bulk material from hoppers, bins, or stockpiles, operating under higher shear and tensile loads than standard transfer conveyors. A feeder shears a layer of material from beneath a static, consolidated head.

2. Feeder Type Technical Comparison

Specify belt feeders for pre-screened or secondary-crushed material (lump size typically less than 300 mm [12 in]), head heights less than 10 m [33 ft] and applications requiring higher extraction speeds.

Specify apron feeders for Run-of-Mine (ROM) extraction from dump pockets, primary crusher feed under deep bins and applications where lump size, impact severity or head load exceeds belt feeder limits.

3. Mass Flow versus Funnel Flow

Undersized hopper openings induce cohesive arching, restricting discharge. Base hopper geometry on laboratory shear testing of the bulk material to quantify unconfined yield strength, bulk density as a function of consolidation stress, effective angle of internal friction and wall friction angle.

6. Pressure Relief Breaks and Stepped Hopper Geometry

In high-aspect-ratio silos, a continuous converging hopper transmits high vertical consolidating stress to the discharge opening under initial fill (active stress) conditions, and the resulting head load can exceed feeder shear and breakaway torque capacities.

Segment the hopper with vertical expansion zones or stepped inclinations to induce bulk solid dilation, which reduces consolidation strength and the active vertical stress at the feeder interface.

7. Dead Bed Protection (Sacrificial Material Cushion)

Maintain a dead bed of material above the discharge opening during normal operation to attenuate impact energy. Direct free-fall onto an empty hopper can fracture wear liners, damage conveyor belts and apron pans, and initiate fatigue cracking.

11.3 Top Cover

Belt top cover is a wear allowance plus an impact-absorption layer. Size it from a measured or benchmarked wear rate and the target replacement interval, then check that not less than 1.5 mm [0.06 in] of cover remains above the breaker or carcass at end of life. Do not exceed a top-to-bottom cover ratio of approximately 3:1 on fabric belts. Higher ratios promote cupping and edge lift once the belt takes a set.

Specify abrasion-resistant cover grades as a minimum. Use AS 1332 grade N / ISO 14890 L / DIN 22102 grade Y for normal duty, and AS 1332 grade M / ISO 14890 H / DIN 22102 grade X for cutting, gouging and impact from large lumps. Use ISO 14890 D / DIN 22102 grade W where sliding abrasion governs.

12. Feeder Belt Tension and Drive Power

Feeder drive sizing is governed by two load cases. The initial fill case sets breakaway torque, while the flow case sets continuous running duty.

Under initial fill, material charged into an empty hopper above a stationary feeder develops an active stress field and high vertical stress at the shear plane. Once discharge commences, the stress field converts to a passive arched state in which the hopper walls carry a greater share of the material weight, and the vertical load at the outlet falls substantially. Initial fill loads are typically 2 to 4 times established flow loads.

Time consolidation during extended shutdowns increases shear strength at the shear plane, and refilling over a stopped feeder restores the initial fill stress field. Retain breakaway capacity for the life of the installation, not only for first fill.

Compute the vertical shear plane force from measured flow properties using the Jenike or Roberts methods. Select the gearbox against peak breakaway torque with a service factor of 1.5 to 2.0 for feeder duty, and verify the motor, coupling and holdback ratings against the same torque, not against nameplate power.
